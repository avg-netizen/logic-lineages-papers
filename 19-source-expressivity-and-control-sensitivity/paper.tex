\begin{abstract}
We study the arithmetic expressible by literal canonical
Wilken--Weiermann assignments of original G\"odel T terms. Finite
Boolean systems of actual source observations compile into one
unconditional ground comparison, with polynomial overhead in a shared
syntax graph. Absolute scalar observers remove restrictions on the
result type, and finite systems of source equations eliminate common
all-one observations of functional variables without an all-one term
inhabitant. For a grammar with arbitrary nested ground binders and
opaque higher-type blocks, we construct exact head certificates at the
original abstraction budget and recover the entire genuine collapse
graph, including its finite prefix. An active functional-argument
example delineates that grammar. Finally, an additive-one perturbation
of the usual control makes ground comparison co-recursively-enumerable
complete, even with only zero, successor, ground binders and ground
inputs. The proofs retain the literal parsing policy and the actual
scalar seeds of their sources. They complement the permitted-control
decision theorem without deciding all-ground comparison for the
unchanged standard control.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Wilken and Weiermann's ordinal assignment for G\"odel's T compares
expressions under every admissible substitution \cite{ww}. Its
canonical instance assigns a finite vector to each literal typed term.
The zeroth component has a natural-number value, while positive
components record ordinal structure. A source program and its
beta-normal form need not receive the same canonical vector.

The companion \emph{Decidable Ground Comparison for Norm-Controlled
Ordinal Assignments} \cite{groundcomparison} proves a decision theorem
for ground free inputs at particular permitted controls. It also proves
functional-input undecidability. The present paper concerns a different
interface: which arithmetic observations can be represented by actual
source terms, and how much the decision problem can change when the
control changes.

Three results organize the paper. First, arbitrary finite Boolean
combinations of source observations reduce to one universal strict or
weak comparison of ground-output sources. This includes arbitrary
supplied source leaves and preserves their shared inputs. The converse
reduction also handles functional outputs, through an absolute scalar
observer. These are exact representation theorems; a compiler by itself
does not decide whether its existential witnesses exist.

Second, functional variables can be observed at the all-one vector
without assuming that a closed source realizes that vector. Two actual
abstractions give a positive-coefficient linear equation for the desired
scalar observation. We then construct unary head carriers through an
entire grammar of ground applications and ground binders. A finite
reserve calculation pays their cost with the original box coefficient.
The resulting certificate defines the whole collapse graph, rather than
only a sparse observable ray.

Third, for every prescribed finite prefix there is a fixed primitive
recursive control differing from the usual example by at most one at
every input for which even a small ground-source fragment has a
$\Pi^0_1$-complete comparison problem. Bounded simulation is placed in
the parity of the control. The source compiler exposes it through the
actual successor graph. Thus growth estimates and close numerical
approximation do not determine comparison decidability.

The source constructions use no arbitrary ordinal-valued primitive.
Every observed head has an actual source or an explicitly stated and
proved carrier certificate. The scalar attached to such a carrier is
retained. The boundary examples and finite-prefix arguments make these
requirements explicit.

\section{Relation to the decision results}

The compiler applies both to the standard control and to the permitted
controls for which the companion proves ground decidability. In the
latter case existential actual-source arithmetic is therefore decidable.
For the additive-one controls of this paper the same arithmetic is
undecidable. These statements concern different fixed functions.

The carrier theorem does not require a certificate for every child of
the source grammar, and it does not enlarge the original scale. Its
restriction to ground arguments is nevertheless substantive. The
functional-argument example changes an infinite coefficient of a
positive partial, which a finite reserve cannot repair. It leaves open
other construction methods for a larger source class.

At the unchanged standard control the full all-ground problem remains
unclassified by these results. A faster closed evaluator, a compiler
for Boolean constraints, or an additional head graph does not settle
their simultaneous existential satisfiability. The countervaluation and
complete-count statements in Section~\ref{sec:boundaries} locate precise
remaining obligations without identifying them with periodic tables at
arbitrary auxiliary heads.
