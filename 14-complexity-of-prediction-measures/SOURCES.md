# Sources and represented-input scope

Samuel Alexander and Bill Hibbard, *Measuring Intelligence and Growth Rate: Variations on Hibbard's Intelligence Measure*, [arXiv:2101.12047v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.12047v1), 25 January 2021, supplies Definitions 2–7, the bounded prediction method in Proposition 8, and Proposition 9's noncomputability result. The discussion after Proposition 9 asks for exact complexity and conjectures a stronger-oracle requirement for an all-total-function list.

The first theorem keeps the fixed effective primitive-recursive listing of source Definition 7 and its strict eventual runtime condition. It needs the listing's effective total evaluator, not a particular numerical index for a named growth function. The second theorem specifies its own canonical least-program-index listing; it does not claim to classify every possible noneffective enumeration.

Standard arithmetical-hierarchy and Turing-jump facts are mathematical background. The manuscript includes the full local upper formulas, actual-history robust-baseline proof, finite-c.e.-set coding, uniform total-predictor reductions, infinite-level calibration predictor, total-function-list construction, diagonal finiteness proof and jump accounting. No private hardness lemma is cited.

The original strict comparator's degree is proved using a fixed infinite-measure predictor. A lower bound for the variant's comparator is not inferred from its value degree. The general E-computable-list statement is an upper bound only. No hyperreal/ordinal taxonomy, unrestricted predictor index or mandatory full-input-reading machine model is silently substituted for the stated contract.
