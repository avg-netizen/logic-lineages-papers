\begin{abstract}
Wilken and Weiermann's analysis of G\"odel's T raises two different
effectiveness questions: a syntactic termination ordering with specified
stability and ordinal calibration, and semantic comparison of the ordinal
expressions used by a particular assignment.  We explain the distinction
and give a common foundation for a collection of proofs concerning both
questions.  We prove an explicit maximal-predecessor algorithm, an exact
single-path evaluator for the collapse, its terminal-count decomposition,
and the forward arithmetic form of actual canonical assignments with ground
free variables.  These results connect typed syntax to an arithmetic problem
whose positive and negative boundaries are developed in the companion papers.
We explain why complete counts, intermediate block counts and finite-state
observers have different decision-theoretic roles.  The remaining exact-standard-
control comparison problem with several independent ground inputs stays open.
\end{abstract}

\section{What the problem asks}\label{sec:problem}

A termination proof and an algorithm for comparing its measures answer
different questions.  A reduction sequence can be known to terminate because
some ordinal decreases, even when the proposed comparison of two expressions
quantifies over infinitely many possible substitutions.  Conversely, a
decidable ordering on syntax can prove termination without deciding the
particular universally quantified numerical relation that motivated it.

Wilken and Weiermann obtain an optimal derivation-length classification for
the lambda formulation of G\"odel's T \cite{ww}.  Their Introduction
anticipates syntactic termination orderings extending recursive path ordering,
with Bachmann--Howard order type.  Their effectiveness conjecture following
Definition~2.8 concerns comparison of their ordinal expressions and vectors
under all admissible substitutions.  These are two mathematical contracts.
The collection keeps them separate throughout.

Three objects are especially easy to confuse.  A general assignment in
\cite{ww} may attach a finite reduction history to an abstraction; the
normalization argument constructs appropriate decreasing assignments along
reductions.  The \emph{canonical assignment} fixes history length zero at
every abstraction, so one literal input term determines one vector.  Finally,
a \emph{syntactic ordering} is a relation defined on terms and stable under
substitution and contexts.  A result about one object is a result about
another only after an explicit interpretation theorem.

The main unresolved domain considered here fixes the source grammar,
the zero-history canonical assignment, and the numerical control
$\Phi(t)=F_5(t+100)$ defined below.  All free variables have ground type;
bound variables, recursors, output types and internal simple types are
unrestricted.  Distinct ground names vary independently.  This domain is
smaller than the unrestricted expression/substitution problem in the
original effectiveness conjecture, but it is still not known to be decidable.

\begin{problem}[Exact canonical comparison]\label{prob:main}
Given two finite original T terms with only ground free variables, decide
whether their canonical vectors satisfy universal weak comparison, or
universal Wilken--Weiermann strict comparison, at the fixed control $\Phi$.
\end{problem}

An algorithm must halt on every input pair, including pairs for which there
is no countervaluation anywhere.  It need not have an elementary or practical
running time.  An enormous closed integer may therefore be computed as part
of a mathematical reduction, but a finite numerical experiment with a smaller
control is not an execution of that reduction on its genuine parameters.

\section{How the companion results fit together}\label{sec:companions}

The collection divides the proofs according to their mathematical mechanism.
The papers are supplied with their complete local arguments and build inputs;
published background is cited conventionally.  The following statements are
an orientation to those proofs, not substitutes for them.

\subsection{A syntactic termination ordering}
The type-confined ordering paper \cite{wwordering} constructs a decidable,
well-founded pair on original T together with a separate finite ranked
ground signature.  It is stable under well-typed contexts and substitutions,
orients the original T rules, and restricts exactly to multiset recursive
path ordering on the ranked ground subalgebra.  Its individual descending
ranks are below Bachmann--Howard and their supremum has that calibration.

The embedding of ranked nodes is part of the statement.  A ranked ternary
node and three curried applications of a native case functional are different
trees.  The theorem does not silently assert exact RPO on the latter encoding.
Nor does it claim that arbitrary linear extensions of the partial ordering
have its descending height, or that the relation is the canonical numerical
comparison of Problem~\ref{prob:main}.

\subsection{Identity and one shared input}
In the residual grammar of Theorem~\ref{thm:forward-translation}, universal identity
has a recursive normal form even with arbitrarily many independent inputs.
The identity/one-variable paper \cite{wwunivariate} proves completeness by
separating distinct open collapse tags on a suitable weighted ray.
For one shared input it gives the stronger effective eventual-sign theorem:
from $E(x),F(x)$ one obtains a threshold $T$ and one sign that holds for all
$x\ge T$.  Closed evaluation handles the finite prefix.

Universal identity is not existential equality.  Distinct functions may agree
at particular inputs, and asking whether several such equalities have a
simultaneous solution is not decided by the identity normal form.  Likewise,
two sources each having one free name do not form a one-input pair when
their names are distinct.  The new collapse-function results concern a
different grammar from the positive exponential terms generated by
$2^f g$; the earlier exponential comparison theorem does not by itself give
the actual-source conclusions stated here.

\subsection{Several inputs in recognized positive classes}
The multivariate paper \cite{wwmultivariate} proves the required arithmetic
hypotheses for the fixed $\Phi$ and then treats finite coefficients,
polynomial heads, and several larger structural classes.  In particular,
recursor-free actual sources are decidable without a bound on internal type
level, and polynomial-head comparison covers actual sources whose maximum
internal type level is at most two.  These are consequences of the generated-
source bounds in Corollary~\ref{cor:source-classes}, not bounds merely on output type.

Common-head layers, balanced heterogeneous layers and single-core rows share
some machinery, but retain different hypotheses.  Their proofs keep every
shared input correlation.  A lower-level core occurring in two arguments
cannot be replaced by two independent witnesses, and several arbitrary core
values cannot be treated as independently variable just because their
individual functions are computable.

\subsection{Actual-source arithmetic and negative boundaries}
The source-expressivity paper \cite{wwexpressivity} defines an existential
language whose additional operations are observations of \emph{supplied
actual sources}, rather than freely chosen ordinal labels.  Its Boolean
compiler turns an existential formula into one unconditional weak or strict
canonical comparison, with the appropriate polarity.  Absolute observers
also handle sources of functional output type.  Thus the remaining comparison
problem and unsatisfiability in that actual-source language are computably
equivalent.

The same paper separates two negative boundaries.  At the unchanged control,
functional free inputs already allow an undecidability reduction on actual
sources.  At all-ground inputs a different theorem obtains hardness after
an additive-one perturbation of the control, even with agreement on an
arbitrary prescribed finite prefix.  The first changes the free-variable
domain; the second changes the control.  Neither settles
Problem~\ref{prob:main} by itself.

\subsection{Exact counts and a moving partition coefficient}
The counting paper \cite{wwcounts} proves the retained-prefix transfer
between actual norm-controlled descent counts and restricted partitions.
For genuine linked parameters
\[
 u=\Phi(v),\quad B=\Phi(u),\quad C=\Phi(u+1),\quad
 d=B-u+3,\quad e=u-4,
\]
one block's parity is
\[
 \delta_4(v)=1+p_d(C-e)\pmod2,\qquad
 \sum_{n\ge0}p_d(n)z^n=\prod_{i=1}^d(1-z^i)^{-1}.
\]
Both the cutoff $d$ and the selected coefficient move.  The complete
Cartier observer retains every denominator factor, and its actual
primitive-root residue tuple has an unbounded exact period.
The theorem is about that tuple and the full state, not a lower bound
for the selected scalar's complexity.

The proof of the growing-budget transfer matters: a constant-budget partition
identity is not automatically an identity for a descent whose successive
budgets follow $\Phi$.  Similarly a count stopped at a first $\omega^\omega$
entry is not the complete count $\tau_\lambda$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:complete-count}.  The latter still includes every subsequent block.

\section{The infinite-search obstruction}\label{sec:search}

Each supplied ground tuple can be evaluated by a terminating computation.
Consequently a false universal comparison has a semidecision procedure:
enumerate tuples and stop at the first countervaluation.  If there is none,
this procedure has no reason to halt.

\begin{proposition}[Countervaluation bounds]\label{prop:counter-bound}
The fixed-control comparison problem is co-recursively-enumerable.  It is
decidable if and only if there is a total computable function $b(Q)$ such
that each false comparison $Q$ has a countervaluation whose every coordinate
is at most $b(Q)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Closed evaluation and enumeration prove the first claim.  Given the stated
bound, search the finite positive box and decide $Q$.  Conversely, given a
decider, return zero for a true query.  For a false query enumerate tuples
until its promised countervaluation is found, and return the largest
coordinate.  This search is entered only when its termination is known.
With no free variables there is just one closed test.
\end{proof}

This elementary equivalence is useful precisely because it does not manufacture
such a bound.  A bound on evaluation time \emph{at a given tuple} is not a bound
on the location of the first countertuple.  Larger ordinal upper bounds and
faster evaluators can leave the latter problem unchanged.

The terminal decomposition gives another view of the obstruction.  On a tail
where $\Phi$ has stabilized residue $r_m$ modulo a fixed $m$, proved with an
effective threshold in \cite{wwmultivariate},
\[
 J_\lambda(t)\equiv \tau_\lambda(t)+r_m\pmod m.
\]
The enormous terminal value has not erased the small residue of the count.
For example, eliminating an independent witness $y$ from
$\tau_\lambda(t)=2y+1$ asks whether an odd count occurs at all.
It is not answered by the rate at which that count grows.

There is a genuinely necessary attainment problem for the complete count
of a \emph{supplied actual unary source}.  Let
$H:o\to o$ have vector $(c_H,a_H)$, put $\lambda=\omega\otimes a_H$,
and $C_H=\norm(\lambda)+c_H+1$.  Its application satisfies
\[
 F_H(n):=\can{HX}_0-1=J_\lambda(C_H+n)-1,
 \qquad x_0=n+1.
\]
For fixed progression and modulus data, choose an effective starting point
$b\ge C_H$ above the residue threshold, with $b\equiv a\pmod p$.
On that progression the query
\[
 \exists t\ge b:\quad t\equiv a\pmod p,\quad
                 \tau_\lambda(t)\equiv r\pmod m
\]
becomes
\[
 \exists k,y\in\N:\quad F_H(b-C_H+pk)=my+s,
 \qquad s=(r+r_m-1)\bmod m.
\]
The finitely many earlier genuine arguments are evaluated separately.
The actual-source compiler in \cite{wwexpressivity} consumes the displayed
formula.  Thus a full comparison decider would decide this attainment query.
The independent witnesses $k,y$ explain why the one-shared-input theorem
does not already decide it.

Certified periodic tables are a stronger proposed supplier for a particular
general elimination route: one needs a threshold, period, exact residues and
certification of all negative occurrence claims on the declared head family.
The necessary supplied-source attainment problem should not be identified
with tables for every auxiliary ordinal head.  Nor has an extraction of the
isolated $\delta_4$ block from every complete source observation been proved.
These distinctions prevent a diagnostic intermediate question from being
mistaken for an equivalent reformulation of the whole problem.

\section{Two small arithmetic lessons}\label{sec:lessons}

\subsection{An exact observer may already distinguish every numerator}
Let $A(0)=1$ and $\deg A=D$ over a field.  On proper numerators the map
\[
 U\longmapsto([z^0]U/A,\ldots,[z^{D-1}]U/A)
\]
is invertible: its matrix is triangular with diagonal entries one.  Hence
distinct numerators have different coefficient series.  In characteristic
two their first differing coefficient has index below $D$ and is selected
by a binary continuation of length at most $\lceil\log_2D\rceil$.
This is the observability interpretation suggested by weighted-automaton
theory \cite{kiefer}.

It does not show that a particular source-admissible continuation distinguishes
the states.  An all-continuations minimizer and a compression preserving only
the one actual remaining word are different problems.  Nor may a rational
Gram-matrix argument be imported unchanged over $\Ftwo$: the column
$(1,1)^T$ has rank one, while its Gram matrix is zero.

\subsection{A successful cancellation in a neighboring problem}
For $N=2^k-1$ and $1\le t<N$, consider the finite set
\[
 \{(a,b)\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}^2:
       a+b\equiv t\pmod N,\ \wt(a)+\wt(b)<k\}.
\]
Swap $a,b$.  All off-diagonal objects cancel modulo two.  There is one
diagonal solution because two is invertible modulo $N$, and multiplication
by two cyclically rotates the binary word.  Its weight is $\wt(t)$.
The set's parity is therefore $\mathbf1_{2\wt(t)<k}$.
Only this elementary fact is used; a full proof of the Tu--Deng inequality
is not assumed \cite{llx}.

The restricted-partition problem has a different indexing set and different
cancellation classes.  The displayed involution therefore supplies no parity
formula for it.  The complete coefficient observer in \cite{wwcounts}
retains its entire generating function; a cancellation law for the selected
coefficient would require an additional argument on that actual indexing set.

\section{What would settle the remaining comparison problem}

The established positive fragments exclude several tempting descriptions of
the open region.  It is not closed evaluation, universal identity, one shared
ground input, arbitrary recursor-free syntax, or the internal-level-two
fragment.  The remaining region combines several independent ground inputs
with nonpolynomial collapse observations beyond the recognized classes.

A positive completion needs a decision procedure for simultaneous existential
actual-source observations, or another mechanism that certifies witness
absence.  A negative completion needs an exact interpretation of a known
undecidable problem using the unchanged control, permitted source syntax and
shared valuations.  The source compiler already supplies the final conversion
to one weak or strict comparison; another conjunction detector is not the
missing theorem.

An unbounded full-state period does not refute a simple scalar law.  An
eventually periodic word is not automatically available effectively.  A
decidable word theory with unary labels does not automatically interpret
addition on its positions.  A source-construction theorem enlarges the
represented arithmetic but does not decide that arithmetic's existential
theory.  These are distinct logical obligations, each requiring a theorem.

The purpose of the collection is to present the established mechanisms and
their exact limits in ordinary mathematical form.  The full fixed-standard-
control, all-ground comparison problem remains open.
