# Source and proof guide

All bibliographic dependencies are public mathematical works cited in the manuscript. The versions matter because the questions and theorem numbering can change.

| Source | Use in this paper |
|---|---|
| Alvir, Csima and MacLean, *Scott Complexity of Reduced Abelian p-Groups*, [arXiv:2407.06940v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06940v1) | Questions 5.1–5.3; definability and upper bounds; Theorem 4.4's reduced-group comparisons. The algebraic extension calculation is Lemma 2.3. |
| Alvir, Greenberg, Harrison-Trainor and Turetsky, *Scott complexity of countable structures*, [JSL 86 (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1017/jsl.2021.4) | General possible-complexity restrictions, Miller collapse and impossibility of exact nonzero-limit difference levels. These restrictions are published inputs. |
| Calderoni and Thomas, *The bi-embeddability relation for countable abelian groups*, [author manuscript](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~sthomas/TFA.pdf), Theorem 4.2, printed p. 10 | Zippin existence with every nonfinal Ulm factor of unbounded exponent. The original Zippin proof is not a separate assumed local document. |
| Gonzalez, Rossegger and Turetsky, *Scott Analysis below the Vaught Ordinal*, [arXiv:2606.15205v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15205v2) | Question 1, prior abstract non-Pi existence and the two Scott-rank conventions. |

Ulm classification and the reduced/divisible decomposition are standard algebraic inputs. The bounded base argument, quotient-filtration calculation, finite-support absorption, terminal-deletion comparisons, explicit decreasing-sequence presentations, and sufficient infinite-divisible-rank comparison are written in the manuscript.

The main reduced theorem uses the bounded base and the two terminal-deletion comparisons. Both comparisons are imposed on the same quotient when excluding a difference sentence. Exact limit lengths and finite tails additionally use prescribed-tail existence and the odd obstruction. Explicit spectrum examples then follow from the classification. The nonreduced pure-Sigma exclusion is a separate argument; ACM's reduced theorem is not silently extended to nonreduced groups.

The infinite-divisible-rank proof is a significant local dependency deserving independent scrutiny. Existing AI checks are not represented as independent verification. The manuscript does not claim a uniform computable presentation from arbitrary countable ordinal descriptions.
