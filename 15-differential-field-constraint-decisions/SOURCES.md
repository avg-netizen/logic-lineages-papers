# Sources, classical inputs and prior-art scope

Russell Miller, Alexey Ovchinnikov and Dmitry Trushin, *Computing Constraint Sets for Differential Fields*, Journal of Algebra 407 (2014), 316–357, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2014.02.032), [arXiv:1208.1152v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.1152v2), supplies the designated-root constrained-pair predicate in Definition 4.3 and the general constraint-set decidability problem. Its T_K is the negative predicate; the manuscript writes Con_Q for the positive one.

Classical inputs are Gauss's lemma, separability in characteristic zero, extension of derivations to algebraic fields, rational function fields, interpolation, Vandermonde coordinates, resultants and Eisenstein's criterion. The short derivation-extension argument is explained, and the generic first-order solution is constructed directly in a localized curve function field. No analytic exponential solution, Picard–Vessiot existence theorem, differentially closed-field existence theorem or named nonconstant base parameter is needed for the local proofs.

The two exact criteria, necessity counterexamples, rational resultant procedure and unbounded cyclotomic family are all proved in the paper. The spectral theorem is a promise-input result. The explicitly recognizable cyclotomic family does not depend on an unprovided general algorithm for recognizing every independent spectrum.

The primary source's Definition 4.3, constraint-set discussion and linear-root-oracle distinction were checked. A bounded web search on 28 October 2026 for constrained pairs, first-order equations over constant fields, linear differential constraint algorithms and the source title did not identify an equivalent formulation of both criteria. It did not establish originality. In particular, the curve function-field argument and the distinct-weight proof are classical methods whose prior use must be considered before any priority claim.

No conclusion is made about all higher-order equations, dependent or repeated spectra, Harrington's extension property, or the full constraint set of Q. The manuscript is an explicit restricted-decision account for review.
