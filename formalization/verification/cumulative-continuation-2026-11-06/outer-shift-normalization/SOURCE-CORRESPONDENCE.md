# Literal outer-shift normalization and all-shift terminal cases

The guard proof begins by peeling fixed outer shifts and terminates at shifted leaves or a nonlinear word against a shifted leaf. This component connects those source operations to arbitrary original literal words.

`splitWord` is a fixed fold over the original letter list. It returns the original prefix through the last non-shift occurrence and the maximal original all-shift suffix. Every original letter, including unused fields, is retained exactly once in the decomposition. `outerShift` is the integer sum of that suffix. `normalizedWord` replaces only that suffix by one canonical signed shift. Empty and entirely all-shift words are included.

The correctness premise is the entire original `WordDomain`, not merely nonnegativity of the final sum. Original domains imply normalized domains and exact values, with a separate theorem paying the original explicit `wordTail`. No converse domain implication is claimed: for example, an undefined negative intermediate shift cannot be rescued by a later positive shift. E occurrences and all positive-rank range counts are preserved exactly; rank zero includes shifts and counters and is deliberately excluded from the range invariant; counter counts are preserved separately.

All-shift/all-shift strict, equality and weak comparisons become literal integer-linear comparisons of the same raw inputs and their fixed sums. The separately frozen additive `LeafWordComparisons` target also covers an all-shift left word against any admitted right word, leaving the first raw input only in a linear scalar expression. It is not used to claim a same-variable decision: that case must use fixed leaf sums or nonlinear growth, not a purported linear atom x<W(x). This includes x=y and the empty opposite word. Source-counter correspondence is inherited only from the checked terminal-growth theorem with its explicit c+d+1 bridge.
