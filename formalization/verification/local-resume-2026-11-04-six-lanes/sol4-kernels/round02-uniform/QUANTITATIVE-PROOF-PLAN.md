## Generic comparisons needed for the aligned seeds

A useful remaining lemma is joint monotonicity of actualScheduledCount for
ordered initial ordinals and pointwise ordered explicit budget streams.
It should follow by ordinal induction on the upper state, the paid joint
maximum/limit-part monotonicity, and the scheduled recursion. No norm ordering
is needed for constant time-indexed budgets. This would be a proved generic
comparison, never an oracle premise of the source uniform target.

For B=f(t), write B-c=d*q+rho with rho<d and theta=omega^(d-1). The paid
maximum identity gives first suffix theta*q+R, where R=M(theta,rho)<theta.
Set A=c+d*q=B-rho. The low remainder's seed can be represented by the
INDEPENDENT sourceValue(d-1,A,t): its first suffix is R, its first total
norm is B, and calibration identifies its remaining actual count. A is
usually much larger than t; no old original-t companion is used.

After removing R, q genuine theta blocks have masses c+d*(q-1),...,c and
entry at least B. Each own child domain holds since c+d*q<=B. The existing
principal-word trace/evaluator can carry their exact value/count data.

For equal parent input and c<e, the c process has at least as many blocks.
If coefficients agree, its remainder has no smaller budget and no larger
retained mass, giving weak seed order through joint scheduled comparison.
If it has extra blocks, one full theta block dominates the other process's
initial remainder R<theta: its mass is <=B-d, whereas the remainder mass
is B-rho>=B-d+1. This again gives weak seed order. Reserve a final block;
the paid two-block repeated-word strict-mass theorem gives a strict input
gap before that final block, provided the region pays at least three blocks.

For cross parent inputs, B'=f(t+1)>4B leaves extra leading blocks. Their
larger seed pays even an adverse mass a<=B': f(B')-f(B)>B' suffices for the
first budget, and increasing integer maps preserve iterate gaps. Joint
scheduled comparison against the smaller initial remainder then yields a
strict aligned seed. All these inequalities still need formal proofs.
