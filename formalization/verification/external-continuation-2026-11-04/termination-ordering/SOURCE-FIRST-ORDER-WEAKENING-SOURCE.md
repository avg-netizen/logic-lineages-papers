# First-order backward computability weakening

Source: 06-godel-t-termination-ordering/paper.tex, type-recursive computability and fresh-context discussion, lines 590–597. This restricted backward theorem does not provide the general forward weakening used by a full fresh-variable argument.

FirstOrder is the literal type predicate: ground is true; arrow domain codomain requires domain=ground and FirstOrder codomain. Thus arbitrarily long ground-domain arrow chains are covered, not higher-order domains. The single target assumes successful shared Context decode, SubtypeClosed, a confined actual term q, and two separate scalar calibrations for Gamma and sigma::Gamma. Computability of the weakened representative implies computability of the original representative at a FirstOrder type.

Proof plan: induction on the actual result type and FirstOrder evidence. Ground uses accepted GroundWeakening backward. At ground→tau, derive original typedness from confinement. For each original numeric ground-computable argument, recover a confined actual ground representative by encodedTypedWitness. GroundWeakening forward gives computability of its weakening. Actual application confinement and canonicalApplication calibration identify the extended application result with the representative of rename(app q a) weaken. The codomain induction reflects that result back. Original application calibration supplies the existential partial-application success required by the old arrow clause.

The extended hypothesis already quantifies over all new arguments, so it may be instantiated at weakened old arguments. No assertion that arbitrary new arguments are images is needed. Partial application success is proved by the accepted calibration, with no sentinel and no numeric code identity. The converse and higher-order-domain reflection are excluded: higher domains would require forward domain computability transport, not presently available. No all-term computability, ID1, transitivity or register promotion. Proof absent pending exact review and freeze.
