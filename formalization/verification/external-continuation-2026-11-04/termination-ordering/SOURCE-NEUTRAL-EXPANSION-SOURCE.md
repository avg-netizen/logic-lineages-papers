# Conditional standard-N neutral expansion

Exact draft for review; no proof or export.

Paper 06 lines 583–589, non-lambda computability expansion. The conclusion uses the actual recursively defined semantic Computable predicate, actual decoded partial application, and the calibrated scalar least set in standard Nat. Neutral means literal isLambda=false, not absence of reducts.

Explicit premises: successful decode of the same context; subtype-closed finite type window; scalar-body calibration; a confined typed term q; non-lambda shape; and computability at that same type of EVERY actual encodedR successor of its representative. There is no Acc-only replacement of this last premise.

Proposed type induction: ground combines canonical scalar carrier with successor least membership in the least-set unfolding. At arrows a computable argument supplies a confined representative of the exact domain type. Apply codomain induction to app q a. Accepted first-edge/residual decomposition classifies an arbitrary positive successor as bottom or a residual descendant of app g a, where H(rep q)(rep g) and g is confined with the original function type. The explicit successor premise gives computability of g, hence of app g a via actual application calibration; the accepted downward theorem handles residual R. Bottom computability is already established and window membership follows from confinement. Argument equivalence is handled by the accepted one-edge theorem, not by inventing a positive argument step.

This is conditional semantic expansion only. It does not establish every term computable, lambda computability, original-order transitivity, ID1 derivability, or global well-foundedness. No accepted source is modified.
