Four supporting targets are checked at their unchanged frozen types:

1. Two actual original Program leaves compute raw source-universe validity and
   target-packed validity on every natural input, for each fixed signature,
   window and Gamma.
2. The explicit guard-normalized step checker equals the unchanged original
   numericStepTest on every natural triple, including rejected inputs.
3. In the unchanged internal calculus, its acceptance is equivalent to both
   actual validity graphs, equality of independently supplied raw outer tags,
   and the old checker's actual output-one graph.
4. Given only the source outer-tag graph, acceptance derives the target
   outer-tag graph at the same symbolic tag and retains both validity graphs
   and old acceptance. The converse holds as well.

The wrapper is a literal Program AST: source validity, target validity and
outer-tag equality are conjoined with the old checker. Every acceptance is
exactly output one. A failed guard returns zero. The target remains decoded by
original decodePackedNumber, with no extra universe filter. No subtype-closure,
valid-window, successful-decoding or certificate-validity assumption is added
to calibration. Internal derivations quantify arbitrary leaves and have no
semantic calibration premise or reflection rule.

The target tag is obtained through original internal Program totality and
output transport, rather than supplied as a premise. The tag decomposition and
transport helpers work under arbitrary ambient hypotheses. This is a concrete
step-level prerequisite, not yet the canonical-typed-source-to-raw-endpoint
bridge. Representative tag transport, compatible typed/argument wrappers,
explicit endpoint construction, application-validity transport and compatibility
with the previously checked normalized checker AST remain to be assembled.
Equal evaluation alone does not identify their internal graphs.

The external selections are fixed-Gamma. Joint-context/context-uniform assembly,
the outer pathValue malformed-object case, CertifiedApplicationLift and the
